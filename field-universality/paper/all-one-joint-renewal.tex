\section{A joint-renewal bound across run boundaries}\label{app:joint-renewal}

The projective-pair bound of Appendix~\ref{app:fresh-pairs} can be
strengthened by retaining the pool of remaining active input labels
across a return and the next run start. We call the result the
joint-renewal bound. For the same $B_8$ encoder at $n=2^{21}$ and
$p=2^{128}-159$, it proves minimum distance at least $224396$
($>10.7\%$), except with probability less than $2^{-46.39}$ over the
two independent uniform permutations. The arithmetic remains
$6.8125n-2$ field additions. This is an intermediate result: the
complete-run bound of Appendix~\ref{app:complete-runs} refines it to
distance at least $245367$ ($>11.7\%$).

\paragraph{A common pool for neighboring returns.}
Fix the quotient by the zero equations imposed so far and a required
nonzero feedback form $f$. Let $\mu$ be the empirical distribution of
the remaining active forms. Every such form is nonzero on a surviving
branch. Write
\[
 u=\mu(-f),\qquad
 v=\mu(\operatorname{span}(f)\setminus\{-f\}),\qquad w=1-u-v.
\]
Let $t=1/\tau$, where $\tau\ge1$. For three nonnegative continuation
bounds $Y,U,Z$, consider the following exposure. Reveal the next active
input. If the feedback stays nonzero, use $Y$ and stop this exposure.
If it returns to zero, reveal the next two active inputs jointly and
use $U$ or $Z$ according as their sum is nonzero or zero. The bounds are
uniform over the quotient and previous nonzero requirements.

For iid labels, the rank-weighted contribution is at most
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal H(Y,U,Z;u,v,w)
  &=(1-u)Y+wt\Psi(U,Z)+u\mathcal P(U,Z;u,v,w),\\
 \mathcal P(U,Z;u,v,w)
  &=U+t\bigl(1-(u+v)^2\bigr)Z\\
  &\quad +(Z-U)_+\max\{2uv,v^2/2\}\\
  &\quad +\bigl((Z-U)/2-tZ\bigr)_+w^2,
\end{align*}
where $a_+=\max(a,0)$ and $\Psi$ is from~\eqref{eq:fresh-pair-lemma}.
To prove this, split the first input into its mass $u$ at $-f$, its other
mass $v$ on the same line, and its independent mass $w$. A return is free
in the first case, impossible on a surviving branch in the second, and
gains one rank in the third. The independent case admits the uniform
bound $t\Psi(U,Z)$ after the quotient update. The nonzero continuation
has contribution at most $(1-u)Y$.

After a free return the quotient is unchanged. If $P_\parallel,P_-$ are
the following pair's proportional and opposite probabilities, its
contribution is at most $U+tZ+P_-(Z-U)-P_\parallel tZ$. On $\operatorname{span}(f)$, the proportional
mass is $(u+v)^2$ and the opposite mass is at most $\max(2uv,v^2/2)$.
Indeed, if mass $z\le v$ is at $f$, the opposite mass is at most
$2uz+(v-z)^2/2$, a convex quadratic maximized at an endpoint. Off the
line, $P_{-,\rm out}\le P_{\parallel,\rm out}/2$ and $P_{\parallel,\rm out}\le w^2$.
Dropping negative terms yields $\mathcal P$.

\paragraph{Distinct labels.}
The nonzero continuation exposes only one label, with no correction.
For a free return followed by the pair, condition the iid triple on
distinct labels, costing $\kappa^2/((\kappa-1)(\kappa-2))$. For an independent return,
expose its label first; the pair on the remaining $\kappa-1$ labels costs only
$(\kappa-1)/(\kappa-2)$. The pair exposure from zero feedback costs $\kappa/(\kappa-1)$.
The budget $\beta(\kappa)=\max(1,\kappa^2)$ absorbs all three exposure lengths:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \beta(\kappa-1)\le\beta(\kappa),\qquad
 \frac \kappa{\kappa-1}\beta(\kappa-2)\le\beta(\kappa),\\
 \frac{\kappa^2}{(\kappa-1)(\kappa-2)}\beta(\kappa-3)\le\beta(\kappa).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Use each inequality only when enough labels remain. Thus a scan with
$s$ inputs has total correction at most $s^2$, even with adaptive quotient
updates. The one-label branch does not consume the two labels used by
its alternative. Prefixes that terminate earlier are covered below.

\paragraph{Gap sums and terminal terms.}
Let $x,y>0$ mark active inputs and outputs. Choose
$\lambda>\max(1,y)$ and give each position a factor $1/\lambda$.
Summing zero-input gaps gives
\[
 A=\frac{xy}{\lambda-1},\qquad B=\frac{x}{\lambda-y}.
\]
The factor $A$ includes the nonzero output on leaving zero feedback;
$B$ reaches an active input from nonzero feedback before marking its
output. As in Section~\ref{sec:distance-proof}, state $0$ is zero
feedback and state $1$ is nonzero feedback. Normalize the zero-feedback
potential to 1, and use $V>0$ for nonzero feedback. The homogeneous
bounds are
\[
 T_0=AB\Psi(yV,1),\qquad
 T_1(u,v,w)=\mathcal H(ByV,AB^2yV,AB^2;u,v,w).
\]
For a rational $0<\epsilon<1$, require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renewal-witness}
 T_0\le1-\epsilon,\qquad
 T_1(u,v,w)\le(1-\epsilon)V
 \quad(u,v,w\ge0,\ u+v+w=1).
\end{equation}

From zero feedback, a terminal prefix has zero or one active input before
the pair exposure completes. Its generating function is
\[
 R_0=\frac\lambda{\lambda-1}
       \left(1+\frac{xy}{\lambda-y}\right).
\]
From nonzero feedback, termination precedes the next active input or
follows a return before the next pair completes. Its contribution is at
most $R_1=\lambda/(\lambda-y)+BR_0$. Put
\[
 C=\frac{\max(R_0,R_1/V)}\epsilon.
\]
Monotonicity, positive homogeneity, and~\eqref{eq:renewal-witness} make
$C(1,V)$ a super-solution for every finite truncation of the trajectory
tree: one further exposure step, together with its terminal terms,
does not exceed it. Monotone extension of the gap sums bounds all
terminal lengths. Extracting length $n$ contributes $\lambda^n$; fixed
input weight $s$ adds the correction $s^2$. Both possible final feedback
states are included.

Keep the same equation span and rank potential across both scans.
For source weight $s$, intermediate weight $h$, and forbidden final
weight $e$ (so the distance is at least $e+1$), permutation averaging gives
\[
 \frac{F_s(\tau)}\tau
 \frac{(sh)^2 C_1\lambda_1^n C_2\lambda_2^n}
 {\binom ns\binom nh\,x_1^s(y_1x_2)^h y_2^e},
 \qquad 0<y_2\le1.
\]
The source moment $F_s$ is unchanged. The uniform continuation bounds
permit the second scan to start in the quotient left by the first.

\paragraph{Rational verification.}
Witnesses are found numerically on a finite grid of pools; the grid
plays no role in the proof of~\eqref{eq:renewal-witness}. For rational
witnesses, the verifier splits $\max(2uv,v^2/2)$ into two cubic polynomials
and homogenizes using $u+v+w=1$. On any triangle of the simplex, the
largest degree-three Bernstein coefficient upper-bounds the polynomial.
The verifier subdivides a triangle into its four midpoint children until
both polynomials satisfy the required bound throughout every child.
It checks only rational inequalities and complete subdivisions.

At forbidden weight $e=224395$, the sparse contribution has base-two
logarithm at most $-46.396269051235$. The dense domain of weight
profiles is partitioned recursively into rectangles; each cell of the
partition is a leaf. There are 154 leaves: 47 use the projective-pair
bound of Appendix~\ref{app:fresh-pairs} and 107 use the joint-renewal
bound above. Each leaf covers all trajectories on its weight rectangle,
so selecting either bound per leaf is valid. The rational binomial,
convex vertex, and upward-rounded summation checks of
Appendix~\ref{app:all-one} apply unchanged. The total failure probability
has base-two logarithm at most $-46.396269051207$. The certificate and
its verifier are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}. This finite
all-message guarantee does not exceed the binary rate-$1/2$
Gilbert--Varshamov benchmark $H_2^{-1}(1/2)\approx0.110028$; the
complete-run bound of Appendix~\ref{app:complete-runs} does.
