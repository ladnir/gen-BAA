# High-characteristic BAA codes

## 1. Parameter regime

The intended instance has length about `N=2^20` and a 128-bit prime field.
Thus `p` is much larger than `N`, but `log_2(p)` is much smaller than `N`.
This regime differs from both limits studied earlier:

- `p` is not fixed while `N` tends to infinity; and
- `p` is not large enough to dominate every integer minor of the encoder.

Fix `N=3K`. Let `B_3:F^K -> F^N` repeat each coordinate three times, and
let `A` be the prefix accumulator. For independent uniform coordinate
permutations `P_1` and `P_2`, define

```text
G_F := A P_2 A P_1 B_3
```

in column-vector notation. Equivalently, the experimental harness applies
the transpose maps to row vectors in the schedule `PAPA`. The same integer
circuit defines `G_F` over every field.

The target remains one-sided inheritance. For a binary distance threshold
`delta_bin`, we want

```text
Pr[d_min(G_Fp) <= delta N] -> 0
```

for every `delta<delta_bin`. The probability is over `(P_1,P_2)`. In the
cryptographic regime, `p=p(lambda)` and `N=N(lambda)` must be quantified
together.

## 2. Why the global determinant bound does not apply

Write `A=D^{-1}`, where `D` is the bidiagonal difference matrix. A maximal
generator minor has a sparse realization with two groups of `N` equations of
degree at most three. Hadamard's inequality gives

```text
|det| <= 2 * 3^(N-1).
```

Consequently, `p>2*3^(N-1)` implies `d_p>=d_2` for every realized encoder.
At `N=2^20`, however, the right side has about 1.66 million bits. A 128-bit
prime does not enter this deterministic regime.

The randomized-prime union bound also misses the intended parameters. It
would require the bit length of `p` to be comparable with `N`, not merely
with the security parameter.

## 3. A sparse dual representation

The appropriate determinant has order equal to the candidate codeword
weight, not the code dimension.

Let `U` be the unimodular integer matrix for the two permutations and two
accumulators, so the row-vector generator is

```text
G_Z = B_3 U.
```

Let `H_0` contain the two adjacent differences in every repetition block:

```text
(1,-1,0), (0,1,-1).
```

Define

```text
H_Z := H_0 U^(-T).
```

Then `G_Z H_Z^T=0`. Both matrices have their expected ranks over every
field because `U` is unimodular and `B_3` has disjoint supports. Hence the
code generated by `G_F` is the kernel of `H_F`.

For `D subseteq [N]`, a nonzero codeword supported in `D` exists exactly
when the columns `(H_F)_D` are dependent. If `|D|<d_2`, these columns are
independent over `F_2`. A dependence over `F_p` is therefore a genuine
`p`-torsion event: every top minor is divisible by `p`, while at least one
top minor is an odd nonzero integer.

This dual formulation yields an absolute distance floor.

**Proposition 1 (large-characteristic floor).** For every realized
repetition-three two-accumulator encoder,

```text
d_p >= min(d_2, ceil(log_(sqrt(20))(p))).
```

**Proof.** Each row of `H_0` has one `+1` and one `-1`. A permutation
preserves its Euclidean norm. Multiplication by the inverse transpose of an
accumulator replaces a row `h` by

```text
(h_0-h_1, h_1-h_2, ..., h_(N-2)-h_(N-1), h_(N-1)).
```

After the first accumulator, a row has either four unit entries or three
entries with absolute values `(1,2,1)`. An arbitrary permutation preserves
this multiset. A second difference has squared norm at most `20`. Thus every
row of `H_Z` has norm at most `sqrt(20)`.

Suppose `d_p=d<d_2`, and let `D` support a minimum word over `F_p`. Choose a
nonzero `d`-by-`d` minor of `(H_Z)_D` whose reduction modulo two is nonzero.
The same determinant is divisible by `p`. Hadamard's inequality gives

```text
p <= |det| <= 20^(d/2).
```

Rearranging proves the claim. ∎

A 128-bit prime satisfies `p>2^127`, so Proposition 1 gives `d_p>=59` unless
the binary distance is smaller. A prime close to `2^128` gives `d_p>=60`.
This guarantee is useful but not asymptotically linear.

The same argument with operator norms handles `L` accumulators. It replaces
`sqrt(20)` by the coarser bound `2^L sqrt(2)`. The sharper constant above
uses the exact two-accumulator row shapes.

## 4. A topological obstruction to large-prime torsion

The sparse encoder circuit gives a complementary bound. Fix the output
coordinates outside `D` to zero and repeatedly eliminate every variable
forced by a degree-one equation. Call the remaining bipartite graph the
residual core. Its cycle rank is

```text
beta := edges - variables - equations + components.
```

All circuit coefficients lie in `{+1,-1}` and every equation has degree at
most three.

**Proposition 2 (cycle-rank barrier).** If a connected residual component is
full rank over `Q` but loses rank over `F_p`, then

```text
beta >= ceil(log_2(p)).
```

The component also contains a nonzero square minor of order at least
`2 log_3(p)`.

**Proof.** Select a nonzero rational-rank minor that vanishes modulo `p`.
Its determinant is a nonzero multiple of `p`. Hadamard's inequality and the
degree-three row bound give `p<=3^(r/2)`.

For the topological bound, expand the determinant over perfect matchings of
the minor's support graph. Fix a spanning forest. Two perfect matchings with
the same nonforest edges would differ by cycles contained in the forest, so
they must be equal. The graph therefore has at most `2^beta` perfect
matchings. Each determinant term is `+1` or `-1`, whence

```text
p <= |det| <= number of perfect matchings <= 2^beta.
```

The cycle rank of the full residual component is at least that of the minor.
∎

For a 128-bit prime, characteristic-specific torsion needs a connected
component with at least 128 independent cycles. The small ternary and quinary
traps cannot survive unchanged at this characteristic.

## 5. `p>N` is not a theorem

The absence of wraparound in one accumulator is insufficient. Two
accumulators can create a determinant divisible by a prime larger than the
block length.

An exact length-six example uses repetition three, schedule `PAPA`, and

```text
P_1 = (0,4,5,2,1,3),
P_2 = (4,1,2,3,0,5).
```

The binary code has distance four. Over `F_7`, the message `(1,1)` encodes to

```text
(5,0,3,0,1,0),
```

so `d_7=3<4` although `7>N=6`. The retained generator has top determinantal
divisor `7`. Its residual circuit is connected and has cycle rank seven.

This event is uncommon but not isolated. A complete length-six census
enumerates all `720^2=518400` ordered pairs `(P_1,P_2)`. For the `PAPA`
schedule, the distance-reducing odd characteristics and their pair counts are

| characteristic | pairs with `d_p<d_2` | fraction |
|---:|---:|---:|
| `3` | 129,960 | 0.250694 |
| `5` | 9,864 | 0.019028 |
| `7` | 2,448 | 0.004722 |
| `11` | 432 | 0.000833 |

No other odd characteristic reduces the distance for any length-six `PAPA`
encoder. No encoder has `d_Q<d_2`. The corresponding `APAP` census finds only
`3`, `5`, and `7`, with 254,160, 28,080, and 1,440 affected pairs. It also
finds no characteristic-zero reduction. These statements are exhaustive at
length six; they do not bound the bad characteristics at larger lengths.

At length twelve, an exhaustive deletion scan over 10,000 sampled encoders
examined 1,493,973 deletion sets below their binary distances. It found the
following odd top divisors:

| divisor | deletion sets |
|---:|---:|
| `3` | 14,442 |
| `5` | 38 |
| `7` | 5 |
| `9` | 45 |
| `11` | 1 |

The unique divisor-`11` deletion belongs to a connected core with 39
variables and cycle rank eight. No divisor above eleven appeared in this
scan. This is evidence of a rapidly thinning tail, not a bound on bad primes.

A structural-aware rerun distinguishes bad supports from distinct encoders.
It also reports every support that is already dependent over `Q`.

| `N` | schedule | samples | encoders by bad odd characteristic | encoders with `d_Q<d_2` |
|---:|:---:|---:|:---|---:|
| 9 | `PAPA` | 100,000 | `3:17998, 5:533, 7:90, 11:5` | 0 |
| 9 | `APAP` | 100,000 | `3:60653, 5:3665, 7:344` | 0 |
| 12 | `PAPA` | 10,000 | `3:1400, 5:37, 7:5, 11:1` | 0 |
| 12 | `APAP` | 10,000 | `3:6691, 5:274, 7:21, 13:1` | 0 |

The `APAP` scan therefore finds a new counterexample with `p>N`. For sample
7,912, the coupled encoder has `d_2=5`. Over `F_13`, the message

```text
(0,0,1,4)
```

encodes to

```text
(3,0,0,0,0,0,6,2,0,0,2,0).
```

Thus `d_13=4`. The retained generator has top determinantal divisor
`39=3*13`. This example shows that the largest bad prime need not decrease
with length. Neither schedule produced a counterexample at characteristic 17
or above in these scans. That observation remains finite experimental
evidence.

The next scan tests selected larger characteristics by exhaustive deletion
profiles. It uses 100 sampled encoders per schedule at `N=15` and 15 per
schedule at `N=18`. Every field receives the same integer encoders.

| `N` | schedule | encoder samples | prime--encoder comparisons | losses | ties | improvements |
|---:|:---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 15 | `PAPA` | 100 | 1,000 | 0 | 30 | 970 |
| 15 | `APAP` | 100 | 1,000 | 0 | 0 | 1,000 |
| 18 | `PAPA` | 15 | 150 | 0 | 0 | 150 |
| 18 | `APAP` | 15 | 150 | 0 | 0 | 150 |

The tested characteristics are

```text
17, 19, 23, 29, 31, 43, 127, 257, 65537, 2^61-1.
```

The scan finds no distance loss in 2,300 exact prime--encoder comparisons.
Except for the 30 `PAPA` ties at length 15, every odd-field distance is larger
than its coupled binary distance. The experiment samples encoders and a finite
prime list. It does not establish a uniform statement over either set.

## 6. What peeling does and does not prove

The new `--stopping-only` mode finds the smallest output deletion whose
sparse circuit retains a message variable after unit peeling. These stopping
sets are field-independent supersets of codeword supports.

| `N` | samples | mean stopping distance | mean binary distance | stopping distance below binary |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 1,000 | 2.612 | 3.131 | 42.0% |
| 24 | 1,000 | 3.998 | 4.862 | 53.6% |
| 36 | 30 | 5.533 | 6.600 | 66.7% |

Pure peeling therefore cannot recover the binary distance. The minimum
stopping cores are nevertheless too simple to carry 128-bit torsion: their
mean cycle ranks were `4.642`, `5.992`, and `6.600`, respectively.

Requiring a larger connected cycle rank raises the deletion cost. At length
24, the minimum deletion averaged `4.84` for cycle rank at least eight and
`7.90` for cycle rank at least sixteen. These finite values do not justify an
extrapolation to cycle rank 128. They show which profile a proof must control.

## 7. Revised proof target

The original small-core proposal was too strong. Harmless odd-field torsion
is common, and a large-prime failure is forced to have a complicated core.
The useful target is a support-resilient saturation theorem.

Fix functions `p(lambda)` and `N(lambda)` such that
`log_2 p(lambda)>=lambda` and `N(lambda)` is polynomial in `lambda`. For a
fixed `delta<delta_bin`, the desired statement is

```text
Pr_(P_1,P_2)[
  some D with |D|<=delta N has
  rank_Fp((H_Z)_D)<|D|=rank_Q((H_Z)_D)
] = negl(lambda).
```

Propositions 1 and 2 remove two classes deterministically:

- deletions smaller than `log_(sqrt(20))(p)`; and
- residual components with fewer than `log_2(p)` independent cycles.

The unresolved range contains mesoscopic and linear connected cores. A proof
must exploit the two random interleavers to show that their integer cokernels
rarely contain `p`-torsion. For comparison, rectangular matrices with
independent entries have strong cokernel and surjectivity universality, and
random regular graph matrices also have established cokernel laws. See
[Nguyen and Wood](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00596) and
[Nguyen and Wood on random regular graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10068).
Those theorems do not apply directly: the BAA matrix has dependent entries,
and the deletion set is chosen after the interleavers.

The next proof attempt should establish a fixed-deletion estimate for the
sparse dual matrix. A bound of the form

```text
Pr[rank_Fp((H_Z)_D)<|D|=rank_Q((H_Z)_D)]
  <= p^(-gamma N) + exp(-gamma N)
```

uniformly for `|D|<=delta N` would survive the union bound over all deletion
sets. Whether the two-interleaver ensemble has this much cokernel
anti-concentration is now the central question.

## 8. Fixed deletions reveal two regimes

The deletion set in a minimum-distance witness is chosen after the
interleavers. It is therefore important to separate two questions:

1. Is `(H_Z)_D` saturated for a deletion set fixed independently of the
   interleavers?
2. Does the same conclusion hold uniformly over every deletion set of the
   relevant size?

The first question is weaker, but it exposes the mechanism that a uniform
proof must quantify. The experimental harness now constructs `(H_Z)_D`
without constructing `G_Z` or the full dual matrix. A requested output basis
vector is propagated backward through the circuit. With two accumulators, the
resulting dual column has at most eight nonzero entries.

The harness applies two peeling rules. Unit peeling removes a column exposed
by a check whose only active coefficient is `+1` or `-1`. A complete unit peel
proves full column rank over every field. Nonzero peeling accepts any nonzero
integer coefficient. Every dual entry has absolute value at most four, so a
complete nonzero peel proves full column rank in every characteristic greater
than four, including the 128-bit target field. Dense elimination is applied
only to the residual core.

For a random fixed deletion at `N=3000`, all 1,000 samples peeled completely
at each deletion fraction in

```text
0.1323, 0.20, 0.30, 0.35, 0.40.
```

The core transition appeared near `0.45`. At deletion fraction `0.45`, 356 of
1,000 samples peeled completely; the other samples had a mean of 1,034 core
columns conditional on failure. At `0.475`, every sample had a core, with a
mean of 1,255 columns. The discontinuous jump is consistent with a sparse
hypergraph core transition.

Small cores still occur far below this transition. At a deletion fraction
near `0.1323`, fixed-set experiments found 14, 27, 6, and 1 nonzero cores in
100,000 samples at lengths 30, 60, 120, and 300, respectively. These four
experiments use different fixed deletion sets, so the counts do not estimate a
single monotone finite-size curve. They do identify the exceptional objects:
almost every residual core had two or three columns and small cycle rank.

The length-60 experiment ranked every such case. Among 100,000 interleaver
samples for the same fixed deletion set, 24 matrices were dependent over
`F_2`, and 18 different matrices were dependent over `F_3` while remaining
independent over `F_2`. No sampled matrix was dependent over `F_5`, `F_7`,
`F_11`, or

```text
p_128 = 2^128 - 159.
```

This is finite evidence for a two-scale statement. Local cores account for
small-characteristic anomalies. A large characteristic eliminates them by
the determinant and cycle-rank barriers. Linear cores require a separate
random-saturation estimate.

### 8.1 Deletion shape is an exact parameter

Let `T` denote the inverse transpose of one accumulator. Its columns satisfy

```text
T e_j = e_j - e_(j+1)  for j<N-1,
T e_(N-1) = e_(N-1).
```

Suppose that `D` has interval runs of lengths `l_1,...,l_r`, none of which
ends at `N-1`. Then `T F^D` is the direct sum of `r` zero-sum spaces on
disjoint coordinate sets of sizes

```text
l_1+1, ..., l_r+1.
```

A terminal run instead contributes a full coordinate space of its original
size. This follows directly from the bidiagonal columns above. After the
second interleaver, the supporting coordinate sets form a uniform disjoint
embedding with the same block sizes. Thus the run lengths and one endpoint
flag give an exact first classification of fixed deletions.

The experiments show that this classification matters. At `N=3000` and
`|D|=397`, 10,000 evenly spaced deletions all peeled completely. Only 2 of
10,000 prefix deletions peeled completely. A prefix core had, on average, 373
columns, 822 rows, and cycle rank 792. Nevertheless, a separate 1,000-sample
experiment found full prefix rank in every tested field from `F_2` through
`F_65537`.

The classification also has an exact entropy count. The number of
`d`-coordinate deletion sets with exactly `r` runs is

```text
binom(d-1,r-1) binom(N-d+1,r).
```

This identity permits a run-sensitive union bound. Few-run deletions have the
weakest peeling behavior but pay much less than the full `binom(N,d)` deletion
entropy; many-run deletions have the opposite tradeoff.

At the intended scale `N=1048575` and `|D|=138726`, one fixed random-pattern
deletion peeled completely under all ten interleaver samples. The fixed prefix
retained essentially its full set of columns under every sample. The prefix
cores averaged 138,707 columns, 302,614 rows, and 298,252 independent cycles.
Their mean row surplus was 163,907.

The prefix is therefore not a small-core problem. It is a highly rectangular
random embedding of a zero-sum space. Treating all deletion shapes through one
peeling estimate would discard this useful algebraic structure.

### 8.2 Why a 128-bit field changes the union bound

For a genuinely random `m`-by-`d` matrix over `F_p`, the probability of losing
column rank is on the scale `p^(-(m-d+1))`. The BAA core is not an independent
random matrix, so this formula is a target rather than an available theorem.
It nevertheless identifies the required strength.

The number of deletion sets of size at most `delta N` costs
approximately `2^(N h_2(delta))`. At `delta=0.1323`,

```text
h_2(delta) = 0.563712...
```

If `log_2(p)=128`, a bound of the form `p^(-s)` survives this union bound as
soon as the residual row surplus satisfies

```text
s/N > h_2(delta)/128 = 0.0044041...
```

The observed prefix surplus `0.1563N` is about 35 times this requirement. This
calculation does not prove anti-concentration for the BAA core. It shows that a
proof may tolerate substantial losses relative to an independent matrix.

The closest general results concern sparse matrices with independent random
supports. Coja-Oghlan et al. give a [full-rank criterion for sparse random
matrices with prescribed degree data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.14090).
Cooper, Frieze, and Pegden analyze [fixed-column-weight matrices over arbitrary
fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09597), including adversarial nonzero
values for normalized rank. These theorems support the core heuristic but do
not apply directly: BAA columns share two permutations, prefix columns are
strongly correlated, and the deletion set is eventually chosen adaptively.

### 8.3 A sharper theorem program

The integer run decomposition and the general multi-reservoir peeling theorem
are proved in [`RUN_PROFILE_THEORY.md`](RUN_PROFILE_THEORY.md). The theorem
preserves a uniform conditional permutation after every earlier collection of
pivots. Its recurrence cannot certify more than 18.90% of the target deletion
for any reservoir partition; the zero-slack fine-mesh limit reaches only
15.22%. The bottleneck is the worst-case charge of four lost singleton rows
per pivot, not the deferred-decision argument. These results support the
following remaining program.

1. For many-run deletions, prove a conditional occupancy estimate that charges
   pivots their typical singleton loss, or bypass complete peeling by proving
   that the residual core has linear row surplus.
2. For few-run deletions, use the random embedding of zero-sum blocks and the
   first interleaver to prove conditional rank anti-concentration. The desired
   exponent is `p^(-s)` up to an `exp(-Omega(N))` error, where `s` is the core
   row surplus.
3. Count near-square cores separately. These are the only linear cores for
   which the 128-bit field does not immediately pay for the deletion-set
   entropy.
4. Combine this count with Proposition 2, which removes every torsion component
   with cycle rank below 128.

This program is more structured than a direct search for bad primes. The
remaining risk is concentrated in a precise class: deletion profiles that
produce a nearly square, high-cycle core while retaining enough algebraic
dependence to defeat the first-interleaver anti-concentration.

## 9. Current assessment

Large characteristic produces a real structural advantage. It eliminates
all low-complexity modular traps and gives a deterministic absolute-distance
floor. It does not make the encoder field-independent merely because `p>N`.

A clean theory remains plausible, but its main theorem must concern random
saturation of a sparse dual matrix. Neither fixed-alphabet type enumeration
nor a global determinant bound matches the intended parameters. The next
mathematical target is a run-profile theorem: peeling or linear row surplus
for many-run deletions, and conditional anti-concentration for random embedded
zero-sum spaces arising from few-run deletions. A uniform count over the
remaining near-square cores would then address all deletions below the binary
threshold.
